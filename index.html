TRIDENT · 海铃 Hai-Ling, "ocean bell"

TRIDENT, the Tropical Deep-sea Neutrino Telescope, is a proposed next-generation neutrino telescope on an abyssal plain in the northern South China Sea, about 180 km from Yongxing Island. It will watch a huge volume of deep seawater for the faint blue Cherenkov light of charged particles made by high-energy neutrinos.

Site
Flat clay-silt plain at 3,475 m depth, slope ≈ 0.01°, bottom currents ≈ 6 cm/s on average
Array
1,211 strings in a Penrose-tiling pattern (70 m and 110 m spacings, golden ratio), with spiral corridors so underwater robots can reach the inner strings
Modules
20 hybrid optical modules (hDOMs) per string, 30 m apart, from about 2,800 to 3,400 m depth: 24,220 hDOMs in ≈ 7.5 km³ of seawater
hDOM
17-inch glass sphere with 31 three-inch PMTs and 24 SiPM arrays for picosecond-level timing
Water
Measured in situ by the T-REX pathfinder (2021, 3,420 m): absorption ≈ 27 m, scattering ≈ 63 m at 450 nm; ⁴⁰K gives ≈ 2 kHz per 3-inch PMT
Goals
Find the sources of astrophysical neutrinos with sub-degree pointing, measure all neutrino flavours (including ντ "double bangs" and the 6.3 PeV Glashow resonance), and test new physics
Timeline
A first phase of about ten strings to validate the technology; the full telescope is envisioned in the early 2030s

Sources: Ye et al., Nature Astronomy 7, 1497 (2023); Morton-Blake et al., Phys. Rev. D 113, 043030 (2026); TRIDENT pathfinder PMT system paper; hDOM design thesis.

What you are looking at

Every event here is simulated by a small generator that runs in your browser. It is built for visualisation and teaching: the events look like neutrino-telescope events, but they are not physics-grade simulation.

  • Tracks: muons (and taus) cross kilometres of water, losing energy steadily and in bursts (bremsstrahlung, pair production, photonuclear), drawn as small cascades along the track.
  • Cascades: electrons, hadrons and tau decays dump their energy within ~10 m, giving a nearly spherical light blob.
  • Light: Cherenkov photons leave at ≈ 42° to the particle, fade with a 27 m absorption length, and are timed at each hDOM. Bubble size shows charge; colour shows time, red first and violet last.
  • Trigger: an hDOM fires a local coincidence when ≥ 2 of its PMTs see light within 20 ns; the event triggers when ≥ 5 hDOMs do.
  • Reconstruction: a straight-line fit of hit times for tracks and a timing vertex fit plus total charge for cascades. These are simple estimates, not TRIDENT's own algorithms.

Approximations: the tiling is regenerated from the published pattern, not the engineering map; the generator uses simple deep-inelastic kinematics, approximate muon energy losses and an analytic light model (no full photon tracking); 40K noise is uncorrelated single hits and bioluminescence is not modelled. Exported data are illustrative and not suitable for physics analysis.

Not an official product

This display is an independent project. It is not produced, reviewed or endorsed by the TRIDENT Collaboration or any partner institution, and it shows no real TRIDENT data. Detector numbers come from public papers.
